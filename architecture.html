lendisk architecture · v0.9

The chosen architecture as of 2026-10-29, ahead of DESIGN.md v0.9. versitygw is embedded in the lendisk process as the S3 front end: it terminates every signature, routes every request, and hands storage calls to a lendisk decorator that captures writes and enforces the lender's policy. Under the decorator sit versitygw's own backends: posix, plain files on the local disk, is the default; s3 re-signs to any S3-compatible service the user names. lendisk writes no signature code and runs as one process.

Status: chosen on 2026-10-29, for now. It merges v0.7 (embedded versitygw, least code) with the "any S3 store" idea, using versitygw's s3 backend as the proxy instead of one lendisk would write. REQUIREMENTS.md v0.9 and DESIGN.md v0.9 are still to be written to match; until then DESIGN.md v0.8 (Garage) is the committed design text. Assumed: a remote store under the local endpoint is allowed with a warning.

What the picture encodes

  • versitygw is the S3 front end, in-process. It owns the listeners, parses every request, verifies every SigV4 signature including restic's and Kopia's aws-chunked streaming form, and implements multipart, listings, versioning and object lock. lendisk supplies the two net.Listeners (loopback and the tailcat tunnel) and an IAMService; it writes no protocol code.
  • Credentials are resolved by lendisk, never stored by versitygw. On loopback the IAMService returns the per-bucket key from lendisk.db. In the tunnel it derives the per-pair secret from the two node keys on demand (NET-7a) and refuses any account whose node ID is not the tunnel peer key. versitygw's own IAM directory is not used.
  • The decorator is the one lendisk seam. It implements versitygw's backend.Backend interface and wraps a real backend. For your buckets it records a pending queue row, makes the call, marks the row done, and turns a not-found into a read-through from a replica. For borrowers' buckets it applies the allow-list, quota, hash verification, immutability and retention. Capture is in-process and crash-safe exactly as in v0.7 (EP-3): no proxy, no second commit point.
  • Two backends, both versitygw's. backend/posix stores plain files at their key paths on the local disk and is the default: one process, no sidecar, no network, and P-5 at its v0.7 strength. backend/s3 turns the same gateway into a proxy that re-signs each call with the store's credential and forwards it to any S3-compatible endpoint. The local store and the lending store each pick one; a mux in the decorator routes by bucket when they differ.
  • The capability profile matters only for S3 upstreams. The POSIX backend has versioning, object lock, checksum verification and conditional writes natively. Behind backend/s3 those depend on the upstream, so lendisk probes them and chooses delete markers or its own tombstones, store-side or daemon-side integrity, accordingly. status labels the weaker modes, as S3-4 already does for external replicas.
  • The replication worker reads through the decorator. No loopback S3 round trip: it calls the backend directly, hashes the stream, and sends it with minio-go to an external bucket over the internet or to a friend through tailcat Dial. One client, one resume strategy, one verifier for every target.
  • A lender can lend space it does not own. Point the lending store at a B2 bucket through backend/s3 and lend that; quota, immutability and retention are still the decorator's. The borrower never holds a credential to it.

Four friends, four configurations

The same binary in four households, each using a different corner of the design. Lending is pairwise and one-way; the arrows are replication flows from a borrower to the lender holding its copy. Carla and Björn have no relationship and never learn of each other.

What the four exercise

  • Every store kind at once. Plain files (Anna, Carla), an S3 server the user already runs (Björn), a provider as the store (Dag), and a provider as an external replica (Anna's B2). Four capability profiles, one adapter.
  • Every retention mode. Store-enforced through object lock on plain files (Anna, Carla), store-enforced through versioning and lifecycle at a provider (Dag, and Anna's B2 with a key that cannot delete), and client-side tombstones where the store has none (Björn). status on each borrower shows which it got.
  • Lending space you do not own. Dag's lending store is a prefix in a bucket he pays for. Björn's quota, immutability and retention are enforced by Dag's daemon, and Björn never holds the Hetzner credential.
  • Pairwise trust (P-2). Anna and Björn are mutual, two invites. Every other pair is one-way. Carla and Björn share no relationship and appear nowhere in each other's state, even though both hold data for Anna and one of them holds data for Dag.
  • Every network case. Hole-punched (Anna to Carla), relayed through DERP because of CGNAT (Anna to Björn, shown as such in both nodes' status), and a static public address dialled directly (everyone to Dag).
  • Evict mode in the wild. Björn's 300 GB of photos keep about 10 GB locally; a restore reads packs through from Anna or Dag. Anna, holding Björn's packs, cannot read them (P-3) and cannot delete them early (RET-2).
  • Recovery for each. Anna rebuilds from her phrase and Carla's address. Dag rebuilds from his phrase and Anna's address, and his manifest brings back the Hetzner credential. Carla has nothing to recover: her store is readable with restic -r /mnt/usb/lendisk/lent-anna-laptop on any machine, with nothing running.

Five ways back to your data

  1. Normal. restic restore against 127.0.0.1:9000; the local store serves it, read-through fills any gap.
  2. From a friend, directly. lendisk peer forward B 127.0.0.1:9100, then restic against that port; B's embedded gateway serves B's lending store.
  3. After total loss. lendisk recover with the phrase and one lender's address; the manifest brings back peers, buckets, and the credentials of every store, including a remote one.
  4. With no lendisk at all. On the default store, restic -r <dir>/laptop on the plain files, on your disk or on the lender's. Behind backend/s3, any S3 client with the store's credential.
  5. From a disk in the post. lendisk export on either side writes the tool's own layout; restic -r /mnt/usb/laptop opens it.

Open before this becomes DESIGN v0.9

  • embedgw takes a net.Listener. Needed for the tailcat tunnel. Fallback: bind a second loopback port and forward the tunnel listener to it in-process.
  • The decorator can see the authenticated account. Needed to root borrowers' buckets and count quota per borrower. versitygw 1.8 lets embedgw take an external IAM implementation; whether the account reaches the backend call is the question. Fallback: a vendored patch carried as a Go module replace.
  • backend/s3 pass-through. Multipart and checksums are documented; whether versioning, object lock and conditional headers pass to the upstream unchanged is not. The capability probe runs through the decorator, so it measures the pair, not the upstream alone.
  • Two backends in one gateway. One RunVersityGW per process takes one backend; the decorator must mux by bucket when the local store and the lending store differ. Small, but it is lendisk code in the hot path.
  • backend.Backend churn. versitygw ships a minor release roughly monthly. The interface is pinned and vendored, the decorator is the only file touching it, and CI runs restic and Kopia end to end on every bump. This is the risk that made the sidecar attractive; the price of avoiding it was the signature code.
  • Listing at 500k objects on the POSIX backend (G-3) and Object Lock semantics on it, both unmeasured, both testable in a day.
